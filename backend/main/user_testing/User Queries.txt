User query : For estimated procurement value exceeding 90,000, what is the procurement approach?, answer : ## Procurement Approach for Estimated Value Exceeding S$90,000

For estimated procurement values exceeding S$90,000, the procurement approaches are as follows:

- **Tender**
  - **Tender Lite** (not exceeding S$1,000,000)
  - **Tender** (exceeding S$1,000,000)

### Description of Tender Approaches:
1. **Open Tender**
   - Tender notice published openly on GeBIZ inviting any interested supplier to bid according to the specified requirements.
   
2. **Selective Tender**
   - A 2-stage process where interested suppliers are shortlisted based on their capabilities via an open pre-qualification exercise. Shortlisted suppliers are then invited to submit their bids.
   
3. **Limited Tender**
   - One or a few selected suppliers will be invited to tender.

This structured approach ensures transparency and value for money in public spending.

User query : What happens if a supplier performs poorly or abandons a government contract?, answer : ### Consequences of Poor Performance or Abandonment of a Government Contract

- **Debarment**: Suppliers who perform poorly or abandon a government contract may face debarment. This means they can be barred from participating in future government tenders.
  
- **Duration of Debarment**: If the Standing Committee on Debarment (SCOD) approves a recommendation for debarment, the supplier or employee will be informed and can be banned from participating in government tenders for a period ranging from **one to five years**, depending on the severity of the default.

- **Grounds for Debarment**: Specific grounds for debarment include:
  - Abandonment or termination of contract
  - Poor performance reports
  - Use of sub-standard materials
  - Corruption and other serious infractions

This process is part of the government's efforts to maintain accountability and integrity in procurement practices.

- **Listing of Indicative Government Procurement Opportunities**: This listing outlines potential procurements that the public sector is likely to conduct in the upcoming financial year.   - Description
  - Financial Year
  - Agencies
  - Procurement Type (e.g., Construction Services, Goods, Services)

  - Projected Procurement Value

User query : What should a supplier do if they disagree with the outcome of a tender they participated in?
Answer : To address a disagreement with the outcome of a tender, a supplier should follow these steps:

- **Contact the Procuring Agency**: 
  - Start by reaching out to the agency that conducted the procurement for clarifications regarding why their bid was not successful. The agency is obliged to provide an explanation upon receiving a written request.

- **Provide Feedback to the Ministry of Finance (MOF)**: 
  - If the supplier has concerns about the Quotation or Tender, they can direct their feedback to the MOF, which oversees procurement policy for the entire government. This can be done via email at mof_qsm@mof.gov.sg.

- **Exhaust All Avenues**: 
  - Suppliers must first attempt to resolve their concerns with the relevant agency before escalating the issue.

- **Formal Complaint to the Government Procurement Adjudication Tribunal**: 
  - If all other avenues have been exhausted and the supplier still wishes to pursue the matter, they may raise a formal complaint with the Government Procurement Adjudication Tribunal.

These steps ensure that suppliers can voice their concerns and seek resolution in a structured manner.
